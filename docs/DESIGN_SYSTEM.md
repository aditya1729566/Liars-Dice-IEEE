# BLUFF.EXE interface direction

The audience is a visitor making fast, public decisions at a local IEEE exhibition. The play screen's job is to show the hidden AI hand, the visitor's five dice, the current claim, the score, and the two legal choices without visual noise. The research screen's job is to show exactly what the AI could know and what it did after a match ends.

The visual system is a precise digital scorecard: paper `#f3f6f9`, white `#ffffff`, ink `#142534`, cobalt `#204bc5`, challenge red `#c73f38`, and border `#d4dee7`. Space Grotesk handles readable headings and interface text; DM Mono identifies rules, probabilities, and provenance. Flat pip dice are DOM/CSS elements, so the same game is playable without WebGL. The current claim is the visual center; the two five-die hands form the top and bottom of the board.

The [Liar's Dice online game page](https://liars-dice.club/) shows a bid, concealed opponents, and a player's hand together on one table, including a phone layout. We use that information hierarchy, while drawing our own scorecard UI and following our distinct quantity-only, fixed-five, score-to-five rules. [Board Game Arena's Perudo page](https://en.boardgamearena.com/news?id=176) reinforces the reveal as the decisive round moment, but its rules differ; BLUFF.EXE does not adopt face-ranked raises, wild dice, or dice elimination.

Interactions use plain verbs: Start match, Raise the bid, Challenge the claim, Next round, Review AI decisions. Keyboard focus remains visible, selectors have labels, primary targets are at least 44 pixels high, and the layout collapses to a single column on narrow screens. The match journal is available only after a completed match; it distinguishes binomial truth priors, sampled policy weights, and revealed outcomes.
