# Research methods

All experiments must identify `fixed5_score5_quantity_only_v1`. Previous dice-elimination and lexicographic-bid runs are preserved but excluded from current-rule results. Current evaluations use deterministic seeds, seat swaps, full per-match and per-decision SQLite records, binary checksums, and Wilson confidence intervals. The initial baseline league compares random, probability, and aggressive heuristics only; it cannot establish game-theoretic optimality. No CFR or adaptive policy may be promoted without training on the fixed-five quantity-only game and a separate held-out evaluation.

## Fixed-five candidate protocol, registered before the holdout finished

The `fixed5_cfr_100k` policy is an imperfect-recall, one-round abstraction trained for 100,000 external-sampling iterations. It permits opening bids at quantity 1 and then quantity +1 with the current or private modal face. This is a legal subset of the full game, but it does not solve the full match. The engine falls back to the probability heuristic when a policy information set or action is unavailable. Its `policy_hit` count records decisions actually sampled from the policy.

The adaptive candidate counts only prior revealed rounds where its opponent was the final bidder, estimates false-claim frequency with a Beta(1,1) prior, and samples frozen CFR on policy-covered decisions until at least three such observations. Thereafter it uses the model-adjusted probability heuristic for 25% of policy-covered decisions and otherwise samples frozen CFR. Missing policy entries use the heuristic fallback. The model shifts the naive truth prior by at most 0.3 toward calling observed overbluffers or trusting observed underbluffers; the result is clipped to [0, 1]. It cannot force an automatic challenge. The model resets on each new match. This sparse, stepwise blend is experimental; adaptation speed and opponent-shift robustness require a fresh evaluation after the seat-symmetry fix.

The primary held-out comparison is seat-balanced match win rate against the common league. The provisional incumbent is the probability heuristic, which led the initial three-agent baseline. A replacement champion requires a positive lower bound on a paired 95% confidence interval for its improvement over the incumbent across a preregistered varied league, zero invalid actions, and no opponent-specific win rate below 40% against strong or out-of-distribution opponents. The adaptive configuration additionally needs a positive lower bound versus frozen CFR and no greater than 5 percentage-point loss versus the probability incumbent in a robust-opponent pool. Inconclusive results retain the incumbent. The 200-per-seat screening run is descriptive and cannot establish a promotion by itself.

Training seed 1729 and evaluation seed offset 4,000,000 are disjoint. Policy and engine SHA-256 values are frozen in each manifest. The evaluation records every decision and reveal, with observed CLI latency including process startup. No exact full-game exploitability claim is made.

The quantity-only one-die toy (`quantity_n1_exact_2000_v3`) enumerates all 36 private-die deals and full histories. Its separate best-response solver integrates over the hidden opponent die and selects one action per information set, rather than choosing after seeing that die. Toy NashConv is the sum of player 0's and player 1's best-response utilities against the opponent's average strategy. The measured value after 2,000 tabular CFR iterations is 0.0017316113; the 10–2,000 iteration convergence curve and source run IDs are in `docs/plots/quantity-n1-convergence.svg` and `docs/quantity-n1-curve.json`. This validates the toy solver workflow only; it is not a five-dice exploitability bound.

## Confirmatory league frozen after screening, before running it

Run `fixed5_confirmatory_1000` uses 1,000 matches per seat and paired seeds beginning at 6,000,000 for `cfr`, `probability`, `conservative`, and `high_call`. No policy or heuristic parameters are changed after the screening runs. The primary metric is balanced match win rate against conservative and high-call, comparing CFR with the probability incumbent on the same seeds and seats. A paired bootstrap interval resamples seed blocks, preserving both seats and both opponent outcomes together. Direct CFR-versus-probability wins and the opponent-specific collapse threshold are additional gates. The run also logs all cross-pairings, so it can expose nontransitive behavior. Adaptive remains experimental because neither screening direct comparison separated it from frozen CFR.

After finding the historical adaptive seat-symmetry bug and a threshold adjustment that could not change a decision at the discrete five-dice priors, the adaptive source was corrected without changing the frozen CFR policy. `fixed5_adaptive_corrected_400` is registered before execution: 400 matches per seat, seed offset 8,000,000, adaptive-focused pairings versus CFR, probability, conservative, high-call, and a strategy-switching heuristic. Its primary promotion gate is a direct adaptive-versus-CFR Wilson lower bound above 50%; robustness also requires no greater than five percentage-point degradation versus the probability incumbent on strong opponents. Otherwise adaptive remains experimental. Historical adaptive rows are excluded from this gate.

The strategy-switching heuristic uses the aggressive baseline through round 3 and the conservative baseline from round 4 onward, without observing private opponent dice. This is an out-of-distribution opponent-shift probe, not a selectable visitor opponent.
